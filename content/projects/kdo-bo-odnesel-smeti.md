Raziskava zanemarjenih ulic v Belfastu, ki išče, kako prebivalce iz pasivnih uporabnikov ulice spremeniti v njene skrbnike.

## Koncept

Opazovanje je pokazalo, da se ljudje na ulici ustavijo le, kadar morajo: na avtobusni postaji, pri rdeči luči ali ob vstopu v avto. Gosto naseljeno območje, kjer večina prebivalcev živi v najemniških stanovanjih, zaznamujejo zaprti in navznoter obrnjeni pragovi. Vrata se odpirajo neposredno na pločnik, prehodi med vrstnimi hišami so temni in odmaknjeni od pogledov, zadnja dvorišča obdajajo visoki zidovi, večino ulice pa zasedajo avtomobili.

Iz analize izhaja sedem principov pripadnosti ulici: udobje in predvidljivost, vzdrževanje in skrb, mehki robovi, oči na ulici, socialni mikroprostori, znaki teritorija in soustvarjanje prostora. Skupaj opisujejo pot od pasivnih uporabnikov do skrbnikov prostora.

Predlog ulico zapre za promet – dostopna ostane le intervencijskim in dostavnim vozilom – in jo spremeni v glavni javni prostor soseske. Pred vhodi nastane prehodno območje s klopjo, drevesom in zeleno pregrado med oknom in ulico, ki mehča mejo med zasebnim in javnim. Nekdanja zadnja dvorišča postanejo prožen prostor za prebivalce, ki je lahko zaprt, delno odprt proti sosednjim hišam ali popolnoma povezan v skupno dvorišče.

# English

Research into neglected streets in Belfast, asking how residents can be turned from passive users of a street into its caretakers.

## Concept

Observation showed that people stop in the street only when they have to: at a bus stop, at a red light or getting into a car. The densely populated area, where most residents rent their homes, is marked by closed, inward-looking thresholds. Front doors open straight onto the pavement, the alleys between terraced houses are dark and out of sight, back yards are surrounded by high walls, and most of the street is taken up by cars.

The analysis led to seven principles of belonging to a street: comfort and predictability, maintenance and care, soft edges, eyes on the street, social micro-spaces, signs of territory, and the co-creation of space. Together they describe the path from passive users to caretakers of a place.

The proposal closes the street to traffic – only emergency and delivery vehicles can still enter – and turns it into the neighbourhood's main public space. In front of the entrances, a transitional zone with a bench, a tree and a green screen between window and street softens the boundary between private and public. The former back yards become a flexible space for residents, which can be closed, partly opened towards the neighbouring houses or fully joined into a shared yard.
