Humanistična disertacija o tem, kako vsakdanje arhitekturne odločitve – stopnica, kjer bi lahko bil raven dostop, nekoliko preozek hodnik, preveč bleščeča luč – tiho odločajo, kdo se v prostoru giblje brez težav in kdo se mu mora prilagajati. Celotna knjižica je na voljo za prenos.

## Koncept

Disertacija raziskuje vsakdanje ovire v domačem okolju, ki naj bi bilo prostor varnosti, domačnosti in nadzora. Razvršča jih v tri skupine – fizične, kognitivne in čutilne – in pokaže, da izključevanje redko izhaja iz ene same napake, temveč iz seštevka majhnih, standardnih odločitev.

Okvir preizkusi na študiji primera: študentskem domu OAK5 v naselju Elms v Belfastu. Ker se stanovalci pogosto menjajo, mora podpirati zelo različna telesa, sposobnosti in navade. Za vsako opaženo oviro disertacija navede njen vpliv in mogočo prilagoditev.

Vključujoče oblikovanje tako ni dodatna plast ali kompromis, temveč vprašanje pozornosti – premik od prostorov, ki predpostavljajo »običajnega« uporabnika, k prostorom, v katerih je upoštevanje razlik standard.

# English

A humanities dissertation on how everyday architectural decisions – a step where there could be level access, a corridor slightly too narrow, a light too glaring – quietly decide who moves through a space with ease and who has to adapt to it. The full booklet is available to download.

## Concept

The dissertation looks at everyday barriers in the home, the place that is meant to offer safety, familiarity and control. It sorts them into three groups – physical, cognitive and sensory – and shows that exclusion rarely comes from a single mistake, but from the sum of small, standard decisions.

The framework is tested on a case study: the OAK5 student residence at Elms Village in Belfast. Because its residents change often, it has to support very different bodies, abilities and habits. For each barrier observed, the dissertation sets out its impact and a possible adjustment.

Inclusive design is thus not an extra layer or a compromise, but a matter of attention – a shift from spaces that assume an "ordinary" user to spaces in which taking differences into account is the standard.
